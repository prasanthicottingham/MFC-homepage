Parents today are spending more time on childcare than did previous generations. At the same time, the US Surgeon General has declared a mental health crisis among American parents. The  Mutually Fulfilling Cities Project offers the concept of mutual fulfilment as an anchor point for embedding caring city goals in the practice of urban planners and designers. The research-to-date asks: what spatial types and design components enable the need fulfillment of primary caregivers while they perform care in public space, and how can they best be distributed at the city-wide and site-specific scales? To answer this, the intiatial research utilized a survey of caregivers and an autoethnographic study based in the greater Boston area.  The resulting work proposes a practical framework for spatializing the concept of mutual fulfilment so that public spaces care for caregivers, while caregivers care for children.
